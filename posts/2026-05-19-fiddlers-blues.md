Fiddler’s Blues from Lonnie Robertson (January 8, 1908 – 1981). Robertson was a Missouri-born old-time fiddler widely regarded as one of the finest in his state. He grew up in the Ozarks and inherited his father’s fiddle after his father passed, going on to play at barn dances across the countryside. He built a career performing on radio stations across the Midwest, eventually hosting programs in South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas, and beyond. He and his wife Thelma were longtime members of the KWTO staff in Springfield, Missouri, and he recorded prolifically on his own Caney Mountain Records label. Historian Charles Wolfe noted that for many people in south Missouri, “the name Lonnie Robertson has become synonymous with old-time fiddling.”

“Fiddler’s Blues” is a beautiful old-time waltz in the key of A. Robertson’s recorded version dates to 1979, and it appears on the Rounder CD Lonnie’s Breakdown (1996). It is unclear whether he composed it himself, but it is quite possible. Many old-time players in the Portland scene came to know it through Stephen “Sammy” Lind of Foghorn Stringband, who brought it to countless jams and square dances in the early days of the PDX old-time community.
