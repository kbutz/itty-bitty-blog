*The Blacktongue Thief* by Christopher Buehlman is a funny, violent, weird fantasy adventure about a young thief who gets caught up in a much larger conflict involving witches, goblins, giants, a very unpleasant thieves' guild, and an increasingly complicated collection of magical tattoos.

**SPOILERS BELOW**

## Chapter-by-Chapter Summary

### Chapter 1 — The Forest of Orphans

Kinch Na Shannack is part of a gang of highway robbers waiting for a traveler in the Forest of Orphans. Their target turns out to be Galva, an Ispanthian warrior who is accompanied by a giant war corvid. The ambush goes very badly. Galva easily defeats the entire gang but spares Kinch after complimenting his archery.

### Chapter 2 — The Bee and Coin

Kinch heads into Cadoth and wins some money playing Towers. He spots Galva again at the tavern and gets slapped by someone invoking the Guild-gift because of the debtor's tattoo on his face. Kinch responds by picking the man's pocket and stealing a valuable goblin-silver ring, then receives a threatening message from the Takers Guild demanding another payment on his debt.

### Chapter 3 — Tick-Turd

Kinch tries to sell the stolen ring to a pawnbroker. He realizes the pawnbroker is setting a trap, uses a sleep spell to expose the hidden accomplice, and negotiates a much better price by threatening to take his business elsewhere. He then heads to the Takers Guild hall to make his payment.

### Chapter 4 — The Hanger's House

At the Guild hall, Kinch is shown a magical vision of giants attacking Hrava, the capital of Oustrim. The Guild orders him to follow Galva west and eventually await further instructions. Kinch is given a cover story about searching the ruins for magical artifacts, but he knows there is more to his assignment.

### Chapter 5 — A Fox to Run With

Kinch breaks into Galva's room and examines her weapons while she sleeps. She wakes up, catches him, and throws him out of a third-story window. Kinch saves himself with magic, climbs back into the room, and convinces Galva that having him along might be useful. She finally tells him her name.

### Chapter 6 — The Wasted Plum

News arrives that Hrava has fallen, confirming the Guild's magical vision. The town reacts with fear because memories of the Goblin Wars are still very fresh. Kinch realizes that whatever is happening in Oustrim is much bigger than a normal Guild assignment.

### Chapter 7 — The Skinny Woman's Bride

Galva agrees to travel with Kinch and explains that she is searching for a lost princess. She also explains that her giant war corvid is named Dalgatha, after the goddess of death whom she serves. Kinch continues feeding Galva the Guild's cover story while hiding his own abilities as a Cipher.

### Chapter 8 — Bully Boy

Kinch watches a play about King Kalith murdering his brother and taking the Ispanthian throne. The story explains how Princess Mireya survived by pretending to be mentally unstable after her uncle's coup. After the play, Kinch rescues a blind cat from cat-catchers, names him Bully Boy, and gets arrested for his trouble.

### Chapter 9 — Fiddle and Famine

Kinch escapes his cell to recover his stolen fiddle and runs into his former associates. When he returns to his cell, he discovers that his cellmate is a Famine, one of the highest ranks in the Takers Guild. The Famine reveals that the prison is secretly controlled by the Guild and helps Kinch escape so he can continue his mission.

### Chapter 10 — The Horsegroom

Kinch tracks Galva down and spies on her at an inn. He discovers that her body is covered in magical tattoos. He also overhears a hilarious misunderstanding involving the inn's "horsegroom" service before Galva catches him and throws him into her bath. Bully Boy conveniently turns up again.

### Chapter 11 — The Lady of Sourbrine

Kinch and Galva leave Cadoth with Bully Boy. Galva decides they need to make a detour to visit the witch Deadlegs. Along the way they encounter Baroness Seldra, who is riding one of the world's increasingly rare surviving horses, and learn that a dangerous mixling named Hornhead is being hunted nearby.

### Chapter 12 — The Downward Tower

They reach Deadlegs's strange tower in the Snowless Wood. The magical vines covering the tower attack Kinch when he tries to climb them, but eventually he and Galva reach an entrance near the top. A young Galtish girl lets them inside.

### Chapter 13 — Deadlegs

Inside the tower, Kinch discovers that gravity behaves strangely and that the entire place seems to run on bizarre magic. He meets Guendra Na Galbraith, known as Deadlegs, and learns that she is the young woman's great-aunt. Galva reveals that she serves Mireya, the true Ispanthian heir, which pleases Deadlegs. Kinch is put into a trance while Deadlegs removes her own rotten magical legs and replaces them with the legs of a corpse.

### Chapter 14 — Witchling

Deadlegs gives Galva a magical staff that can transform into a clockwork horse for an hour. She introduces her great-niece Norrigal, a young witch-in-training who will join the journey. Deadlegs also performs a ritual with Kinch and Norrigal and warns Kinch that the Magickers Guild is secretly controlled by the Takers Guild.

### Chapter 15 — The Charcoal Makers

The group finds three murdered charcoal makers and follows enormous tracks belonging to Hornhead. They decide to pursue him both because he is dangerous and because there is a bounty on him. Norrigal uses magic to track the group, but they soon discover that Hornhead and his companions are waiting to ambush them.

### Chapter 16 — Hornhead

Hornhead's gang attacks. Galva fights Hornhead but initially cannot penetrate the magical tattoo protecting him. Kinch reads the writing on Hornhead's tattoo and realizes it gives Galva a clue about how to bypass the protection. Galva switches hands and kills Hornhead, while Kinch finishes him by cutting his throat. Kinch also realizes that Dalgatha is actually a magical "sleeper" tattoo stored inside Galva's body.

### Chapter 17 — Spoils of Battle

Norrigal heals Kinch after the fight and Galva gives one of the wounded attackers a mercy killing. They loot the dead and prepare Hornhead's head so they can collect the bounty. Norrigal eventually does the decapitation herself when Galva objects. Bully Boy mysteriously reappears again.

### Chapter 18 — Pigdenay

The group reaches Pigdenay, where Kinch spent several years studying at the Takers Guild's Low School. Kinch remembers some of the brutal training he received there. Norrigal tries to collect the bounty on Hornhead but is refused, so she sells his head to a traveling carnival instead. The money is enough to get them passage west by ship.

### Chapter 19 — The Spigot and the Noose

Kinch secures passage on the whaler *Suepka Būryey*. Before leaving, he goes to a public hanging to practice his pickpocketing. One of the condemned prisoners turns out to be the woman from Hornhead's gang, whom Kinch had nicknamed "Deerpants." He watches her execution and steals a valuable ornament from a wealthy woman as a strange tribute to her.

### Chapter 20 — A Knife in the Mouth

Kinch intends to leave Bully Boy behind because he doesn't think the cat will survive a sea voyage. Instead, Bully Boy starts acting strangely and a tattooed woman emerges from underneath the bed. She is Sesta, a Takers Guild Assassin-Adept who has been magically hiding inside the cat. She reveals that Kinch's real assignment is to smuggle her into Oustrim.

### Chapter 21 — Old Friends

Kinch and the others board the *Suepka Būryey*. Kinch gives his hometown when the captain asks where he is from, and one of the crew members immediately recognizes the name. It is Malk Na Brannyck, a veteran of the Goblin Wars who has a long-standing grudge against Kinch.

### Chapter 22 — A Blind Cat's Luck

A flashback explains why Malk hates Kinch. As a teenager, Kinch joined the Takers Guild partly to avoid military conscription. Malk's father was responsible for Kinch's military service and suffered financially because Kinch deserted, then later died fighting goblins. Malk blames Kinch for what happened.

### Chapter 23 — The God I'll Take Today

The voyage begins and Kinch and Norrigal suffer badly from seasickness. They talk about their gods and gradually become closer. When the crew spots a rare red whale, Galva and Kinch are forced to join the hunting party despite having no interest in doing so.

### Chapter 24 — The Whale

Kinch and Galva end up in Malk's whaling boat. Malk continues taunting Kinch about his military service. The crew manages to kill the whale, but only after it destroys another boat and kills a crew member. Kinch is left wondering whether killing the whale was really a victory.

### Chapter 25 — The Kraken

While the crew is towing the whale back to the ship, a young kraken attacks. Malk leads an attack against it, which goes badly. The kraken kills several sailors and eventually climbs onto the ship itself, creating complete chaos. The survivors manage to drive it away, but the danger is clearly not over.

### Chapter 26 — Catch the Lady

The ship processes the whale and finds valuable ambergris. Kinch and Norrigal grow closer, and she tells him more about her childhood and her relationship with magic. Galva and Malk surprisingly become friends while playing cards. Kinch starts thinking about what a life with Norrigal might actually look like.

### Chapter 27 — That Bitch Death's Cunny

Malk finally confronts Kinch about his military desertion. When Malk insults Kinch's family, Kinch challenges him to a duel. Galva steps in and takes Kinch's place, challenging Malk herself because of his disrespect toward her goddess. They agree to fight to the death.

### Chapter 28 — Practical Experience with Poison

Galva is poisoned the night before the duel by Menrigo, one of the crew. Kinch realizes what happened and, with help from Norrigal and signals from Sesta inside Bully Boy, keeps Galva alive. Malk then challenges Kinch instead and throws Bully Boy overboard.

### Chapter 29 — The Tooth of the Vine

Kinch and Malk begin their unarmed duel. Kinch relies on grappling and dirty tricks because Malk is much stronger than he is. Before either can win, the kraken returns and interrupts the fight.

### Chapter 30 — The Pig in the Drink

The kraken attacks the *Suepka Būryey* and sinks the ship. Galva uses Deadlegs's staff to summon the clockwork horse and help the survivors escape. Captain Boltch is killed by the kraken, Norrigal blinds the creature with magic, and Kinch kills Menrigo for poisoning Galva. The survivors row toward an island.

### Chapter 31 — A Galtish Love Song

The survivors reach a barren island and try to figure out how not to die of thirst. Malk and Kinch eventually agree to a truce. That night, Kinch, Norrigal, and Malk sing an illegal Galtish song together, and Kinch and Norrigal begin moving toward an actual romantic relationship.

### Chapter 32 — Spiders Out of String

Bully Boy turns up alive. Sesta reveals that she survived being thrown overboard by using her magical tattoos and once again reminds Kinch that her real mission is to reach Oustrim. Kinch warns her not to hurt his companions. Later, he and Norrigal finally spend the night together.

### Chapter 33 — Fucking Unmarriageable

A goblin ship approaches the island. The group tries to hide all evidence that humans are there, but Norrigal accidentally exposes them. The goblins discover the survivors and attack.

### Chapter 34 — A Shiver of Her Wings

The group fights the goblins but is overwhelmed and captured. Norrigal uses wind magic and Galva releases Dalgatha, but a goblin wizard reverses the wind and kills the giant corvid. Galva is badly injured by the magical backlash. While imprisoned, Norrigal finally explains the truth about their mission: they are looking for Queen Mireya.

### Chapter 35 — The Bright Thing in the Grave

Norrigal tells Kinch the story of Mireya. She is the rightful heir of Ispanthia, whose uncle Kalith murdered her father and took the throne. Mireya survived by pretending to be unstable and was eventually married off to the king of Oustrim, where she became a powerful witch and drove the Takers Guild out of the country. Galva's mission is to find Mireya and restore her to power.

### Chapter 36 — A Death of Seagulls

The goblins prepare to kill Gormalin. Kinch, Malk, and Norrigal sing loudly to drown out the sounds. Later, Kinch discovers that the goblins have been poisoned. Sesta poisoned their salt supply, allowing the survivors to escape and take the goblin ship.

### Chapter 37 — The Fourth Woman

A Middlesea fireship spots the captured goblin ship and attacks it, assuming the survivors are goblins. Norrigal uses magic to defend them, but her staff is destroyed. Kinch manages to convince the crew of the *Fourth Woman* that they are human survivors, and the fireship rescues them.

### Chapter 38 — The First Daughter

The survivors reach Edth, where the Harbormistress does not want news of the goblin attack to start another war. She offers them money and safe passage if they claim they merely found an abandoned ship. Galva initially refuses but then reveals that she is the daughter of a duke and uses her position to secure money for the mission.

### Chapter 39 — The Moon-Wife

Kinch and Norrigal decide to enter a traditional Galtish moon-vow, essentially a temporary marriage lasting one month. They exchange magical clovenstone pendants that allow them to find each other. This is less a casual fling at this point and more Kinch genuinely falling in love with her.

### Chapter 40 — The Baths of Edth

Sesta confronts Kinch at the public baths. She warns him that his relationship with Norrigal is making him a liability to the Guild. She also reveals a magical tattoo that acts as a dead man's switch: if Sesta dies, the Guild will be instructed to kill Kinch's family. Kinch realizes just how completely the Guild still controls him.

### Chapter 41 — The Quartered Sun

Galva formally asks Kinch, Norrigal, and Malk to continue the mission with her and rescue Mireya. They agree, with Malk primarily interested in the reward. Galva explains that they need to find her old swordmaster before continuing into Oustrim.

### Chapter 42 — The Goddess of Second Chances

Kinch finally tells Norrigal about Sesta hiding inside Bully Boy. Norrigal comes up with a solution: she uses magic to trap the cat and Sesta together inside a sleeper tattoo on Kinch's arm. Bully Boy effectively becomes part of the tattoo, with Sesta trapped inside it. Norrigal warns Kinch that Sesta may still be able to affect his dreams.

### Chapter 43 — The Ispanthian Army

The group finds the Ispanthian army and Galva reunites with her swordmaster, Yorbez. They explain the next stage of the plan: travel to Grevitsa, obtain a map of Hrava, and then seek out the magicker Fulvir. They also discuss their broader plans against King Kalith.

### Chapter 44 — A Bone in the Throat

Kinch dreams that he is choking on a chicken bone that turns into a knife. Sesta speaks to him through the wound and threatens his family, trying to convince him to release her. Kinch wakes up horrified and realizes that trapping Sesta inside the tattoo has not stopped her from tormenting him.

### Chapter 45 — Blood to Milk

Kinch watches Yorbez train and realizes just how formidable she is. He also learns that Yorbez and Galva have both undergone ritual mastectomies as part of their devotion to the goddess of death. The group then continues into Molrova.

### Chapter 46 — City of Lace

The group reaches Grevitsa, where humans and goblins live in separate districts under a very tense arrangement. They meet Ched, a thief who provides a sewer map of Hrava and tells them about Ürmehen, a thief who is not controlled by the Takers Guild. Norrigal also buys a magical ring containing a single lightning bolt.

### Chapter 47 — The Pull

A goblin taunts Malk about the Goblin Wars and challenges him to a traditional "pull," essentially a deadly tug-of-war across the border between the human and goblin districts. Malk accepts. The humans initially have the advantage, but when they have to divert their strength to save another person, Malk is pulled across the boundary and killed.

### Chapter 48 — The Bitterns

The group continues toward the Bittern Mountains, grieving Malk. Galva explains that they are going to meet Fulvir, the bizarre magicker who created her magical corvid tattoos. A strange magical fog separates Kinch from the group.

### Chapter 49 — The Golem

Kinch is attacked by a clay golem that mimics Norrigal's voice. He destroys it, only for the golem to reveal Fulvir's face. Kinch then finds himself sitting at a table with Galva and Norrigal, who explain that he apparently just fell asleep in his soup. Fulvir has been testing him.

### Chapter 50 — Father of Lies

Fulvir takes Kinch and Norrigal into his enormous magical library. He claims to be Kinch's father, then admits that he is lying, although he knows enough about Kinch's mother to make the claim suspicious. He gives Kinch books about magical plants, poisons, and tattoos.

### Chapter 51 — Father of Abominations

Kinch explores Fulvir's property despite being warned not to. He discovers cages full of human-animal mixlings, including a fox-headed child associated with his own god, Fothannon. A magical trap catches him, and Norrigal has to use her last dream-walking spell to rescue him.

### Chapter 52 — Bread and Butter

Fulvir announces that he is moving his house because a giant army is approaching. He forces the group to take three strange musicians with them: Bizh, Nazh, and Gorbol. Fulvir's enormous house literally walks away on giant roots as the group departs.

### Chapter 53 — The Oxbone Walls

The group enters war-torn Oustrim and meets refugees fleeing the giant invasion. They learn that Hrava has fallen and that King Hagli is dead, but there are rumors that Mireya survived. Yorbez catches Bizh stealing food and cuts off part of his nose as punishment.

### Chapter 54 — The Dogs of Hrava

Kinch scouts the ruins of Hrava looking for Ürmehen and any sign of Mireya. He encounters starving dogs and a group of street children led by Janliff. The children point him toward the underground refugee community, but Kinch is spotted by giants who are using enslaved humans as hunting dogs. He escapes into the sewers.

### Chapter 55 — The Upright Man

Kinch reaches the underground community and meets Ürmehen, a thief-king who controls the sewers. Ürmehen explains that the Takers Guild helped provoke the giant invasion as revenge against Mireya for expelling them from Oustrim. He knows where Mireya was taken but demands a sexual favor in exchange for the information. Kinch instead challenges him to a game of Towers.

### Chapter 56 — Towers

Kinch plays Towers against Ürmehen and wins the information he needs. Ürmehen reveals that his gang captured Mireya and sold her to the Guild's Full Shadow. Kinch also steals a magical ring that protects the wearer from falling. In one of Kinch's characteristic corrections to his own narration, he later admits that his first version of the encounter with Ürmehen was deliberately sanitized.

### Chapter 57 — Behind the False Wall

Kinch follows Ürmehen's directions to the place where Mireya was imprisoned. He discovers a hidden dungeon and then a Takers Guild escape tunnel filled with traps. After surviving the tunnel, he emerges in the hills and uses his magical clovenstone to summon Norrigal and the others.

### Chapter 58 — Come the Giants

The group follows the Guild's trail and is attacked by giants. Norrigal is struck by a thrown log and both of her legs are broken. The three strange musicians finally reveal their magical nature and use their music to slow the giants, but the remaining giant kills all three. Their bodies transform into mice as they die. The group kills the final giant and carries Norrigal into a nearby cave.

### Chapter 59 — In the Cave

Inside the cave, the group discovers Queen Mireya hiding on a ledge and a dying giant named Misfa. Mireya reveals that she and Galva were once lovers. Misfa offers to tell the group her "death-song," the giant tradition of telling the truth at the end of one's life.

### Chapter 60 — Her Death Song

Misfa explains how the Takers Guild captured her, magically shrank her, and covered her body in tattoos. She eventually escaped when the magicker controlling the spell died. During the chaos she encountered the magical book that the Guild was transporting and later discovered that the bird hiding in the cave was actually Mireya.

### Chapter 61 — The Witch-Queen

Mireya confirms that the Takers Guild deliberately provoked the giant war. She demonstrates how powerful she has become by enchanting a waterskin to produce an endless supply of water. Kinch realizes that Mireya is not merely a lost princess—she is a formidable witch and a serious threat to the Guild.

### Chapter 62 — The Murder Alphabet

Kinch examines the magical book and recognizes it as the Book of the Full Shadow, written in the dangerous Murder Alphabet. Because he is a Cipher, he can read it safely. The book contains evidence that the Takers Guild may have been responsible for the plague that wiped out the world's horses. The book itself is alive and produces a creature from its pages.

### Chapter 63 — Lightning in the Dark

Norrigal destroys the creature with the lightning stored in her magical ring. The magical backlash breaks the spell containing Sesta, freeing the assassin from Kinch's arm. Sesta attacks the group and kills Yorbez, while also badly injuring Galva and Norrigal. As Sesta dies, she activates a magical tattoo that rewinds time.

### Chapter 64 — The Rabbit and the Wolf

Time rewinds and the fight happens again, except Sesta now knows what is coming. She kills Yorbez again, breaks Galva's back, and mortally wounds Norrigal. Norrigal realizes what must happen and asks Kinch to kill her. He does, triggering a spell that brings Deadlegs into the present. Deadlegs defeats Sesta and removes her magical tattoos, after which Mireya kills the now-powerless assassin.

### Chapter 65 — Running West

Deadlegs heals Galva and explains that she and Norrigal are the same person at different points in time. Norrigal will eventually become Deadlegs. Deadlegs and Mireya head east to find the Ispanthian army, while Kinch and Galva head west carrying the Book of the Full Shadow. Kinch also uses magic to release a living horse from Misfa's sleeper tattoos—the first horse anyone has seen in decades. Bully Boy is finally free of Sesta and remains with Kinch.

## Character Summary

* **Kinch Na Shannack**: The narrator and protagonist. A young Galtish thief trained by the Takers Guild, deeply in debt to them and considerably more morally complicated than he initially lets on. He is also a Cipher, giving him an innate ability to read magical and unfamiliar written languages.
* **Galva**: A veteran of the Goblin Wars and a warrior devoted to the goddess of death. She is extremely competent, surprisingly compassionate, and carries the giant war corvid Dalgatha as a magical sleeper tattoo.
* **Norrigal**: A young Galtish witch and Deadlegs's great-niece. She joins Kinch and Galva after they visit Deadlegs and becomes Kinch's romantic partner. She is ultimately revealed to be Deadlegs at a later point in her life.
* **Malk Na Brannyck**: A veteran mercenary and former soldier from Kinch's hometown. He hates Kinch because Kinch's decision to avoid military service had consequences for Malk's family. Despite their antagonism, the two eventually develop a grudging respect for each other.
* **Bully Boy**: Kinch's blind tabby cat. He initially appears to be an ordinary stray that Kinch adopts, but Sesta is secretly hiding inside him. After Sesta is removed, Bully Boy remains as Kinch's actual cat.
* **Sesta**: A Takers Guild Assassin-Adept who is assigned to accompany Kinch without his knowledge. She initially hides inside Bully Boy and later becomes trapped inside a sleeper tattoo on Kinch's arm. She is extremely dangerous and uses magical tattoos as both weapons and tools of surveillance.
* **Deadlegs (Guendra Na Galbraith)**: A powerful and extremely strange witch who lives in the Snowless Wood. She is Norrigal's great-aunt and eventually turns out to be Norrigal herself at a later point in time.
* **Queen Mireya**: The rightful heir to Ispanthia and a powerful witch. Her uncle Kalith murdered her father and took the throne, forcing Mireya into exile. She eventually became Queen of Oustrim and drove the Takers Guild out of the country.
* **Yorbez (Nadalle Seri-Orbez)**: Galva's former swordmaster and one of the most formidable warriors in the story. She joins the group as they prepare to enter Oustrim and is killed by Sesta during the final confrontation.
* **Fulvir**: An eccentric and extremely powerful magicker known as the Father of Abominations. He created Galva's magical corvid tattoos and seems to know far more about Kinch's family than he admits. He may or may not be Kinch's father, although Fulvir's own relationship with the truth makes that difficult to determine.
* **Ürmehen**: A thief-king who controls the underground refugee community beneath Hrava. He is independent of the Takers Guild and possesses information about what happened to Mireya.
* **Misfa**: A giantess who was captured and magically shrunk by the Takers Guild. Her body is covered in sleeper tattoos, including the tattoo that eventually provides Kinch with a living horse.
* **King Kalith**: Mireya's uncle, who murdered her father and took the throne of Ispanthia. He represents the political power that Galva and Mireya ultimately hope to overthrow.
* **Dalgatha**: Galva's enormous war corvid and magical companion, named after the Ispanthian goddess of death. Dalgatha is stored inside a sleeper tattoo on Galva's body and can be released when needed.
* **The Takers Guild**: The enormous criminal organization that trained Kinch and controls much of the world through debt, assassination, espionage, and magical knowledge. The Guild is ultimately revealed to be much more powerful—and much more destructive—than Kinch initially understands.

## Books to Read Next

* **The Lies of Locke Lamora** — Scott Lynch
  The obvious one, but still the right one. Another fantasy about a smartass professional thief who spends a lot of time getting himself into situations considerably more dangerous than he anticipated. The big difference is that Locke is much more of a con artist, while Kinch is more of a scrappy survivor.

* **The Blade Itself** — Joe Abercrombie
  If what I liked was the combination of **grim violence, dark humor, and characters who are considerably messier than traditional fantasy heroes**, Abercrombie is a natural next stop. Kinch's narration has some of the same cynical-but-funny energy as Abercrombie's characters.

* **Kings of the Wyld** — Nicholas Eames
  This is probably the best recommendation if what I liked most was the **ridiculousness** of the adventure. It has the same willingness to throw genuinely funny situations into the middle of a very dangerous fantasy world.

* **The Blacktongue Thief: Daughter's War** — Christopher Buehlman
  Not technically a "next" book in the sense of moving to a different author, but this is the obvious follow-up. It's a prequel told from Galva's perspective and should provide a lot more context for the Goblin Wars and the character who is arguably more interesting than Kinch.

* **Between Two Fires** — Christopher Buehlman
  Worth trying if what worked here was Buehlman's ability to combine **horror, brutality, weirdness, and surprisingly sincere characters**. It is a very different book, but the tonal DNA is recognizable.

* **The Adventures of Amina al-Sirafi** — Shannon Chakraborty
  A somewhat less obvious recommendation, but I think it fits the *feel* of the adventure: an experienced, morally complicated protagonist, a strange crew, supernatural problems that keep escalating, and a lot of personality in the narration.
