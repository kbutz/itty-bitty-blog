*The Dispossessed* (1974), subtitled *An Ambiguous Utopia*, follows Shevek, a physicist from the anarchist society of Anarres, as he travels to its wealthy, capitalist sister planet Urras. The novel alternates between his earlier life on Anarres and his visit to Urras, gradually revealing the compromises and contradictions of both societies.

These are plot-recall notes rather than a review. **Full spoilers, including the ending, follow.**

## Plot Summary

### Two worlds and an unfinished revolution

Generations before Shevek's birth, followers of the revolutionary thinker Odo leave Urras to establish an anarchist society on the harsh moon Anarres. Their ideal is a world without private property, government, or entrenched hierarchy, organized around mutual aid and voluntary cooperation. Yet Anarres's scarcity and its increasingly rigid social expectations put pressure on that ideal.

Urras, meanwhile, is materially abundant but divided by class, wealth, and competing states. The powerful capitalist nation of A-Io presents itself as free and prosperous, while its inequalities and political repression become more apparent to Shevek.

### Growing up on Anarres

Shevek shows an early gift for mathematics and physics. His childhood and education expose him to both the strengths and the limits of Anarresti life: solidarity and shared labor coexist with conformity, informal authority, and suspicion of exceptional ambition. He becomes interested in the nature of time, particularly the relationship between sequence and simultaneity.

As a young scientist, he works with the older physicist Sabul, who controls access to important Urrasti research and uses his position to claim credit and obstruct Shevek's independent work. Shevek begins to see how a society opposed to formal hierarchy can nevertheless develop gatekeepers.

### Takver, work, and separation

Shevek forms a deep partnership with Takver, a biologist whose commitment to living systems and everyday relationships complements his abstract research. They have children, but their lives are repeatedly shaped by labor postings, scarcity, and institutional pressures. During a severe drought and famine, Shevek performs demanding communal work away from his family.

These experiences complicate any simple opposition between individual freedom and social obligation. Shevek values solidarity, but he also comes to question when the language of duty becomes a way to silence dissent.

### The Syndicate of Initiative

Shevek and like-minded colleagues establish the Syndicate of Initiative to communicate directly with Urras and challenge the isolation and intellectual stagnation of Anarres. Their efforts draw hostility from people who regard contact with the old world as a betrayal of Odonian principles.

Shevek concludes that genuine fidelity to the revolution may require reopening it to criticism and change. He decides to travel to Urras, becoming the first Anarresti in generations to make the journey.

### A celebrated visitor on Urras

On Urras, Shevek is welcomed by influential academics and housed in comfort at the university in A-Io. He is fascinated by the planet's beauty, abundance, and intellectual resources, but he gradually realizes that his hosts are interested in possessing the results of his research.

The contrast with Anarres is sharp: Urras provides material luxuries and formal prestige that Anarres lacks, but much of this wealth depends on unequal labor and restricted opportunity. Shevek's hosts celebrate his independence while trying to contain it.

### Discovering the costs of Urrasti society

Shevek's experiences reveal the gender hierarchy, class divisions, and state power beneath A-Io's polished surface. He learns more about political resistance and the suffering of those excluded from its prosperity. A violent crackdown on a mass demonstration makes the state's coercive power impossible to ignore.

Disillusioned with his academic patrons, Shevek seeks refuge at the embassy of Terra, whose ambassador offers a perspective from beyond the two worlds' ideological conflict.

### Giving the theory away

Shevek completes the work that makes instantaneous communication possible, a breakthrough associated with the ansible. Rather than allow A-Io or any one government to monopolize it, he shares the theory through the wider interstellar community.

His decision reflects the book's recurring distinction between knowledge as a common human achievement and knowledge as property. His scientific breakthrough and his political commitment are inseparable.

### Returning to Anarres

Shevek returns to Anarres knowing that he may face hostility and that he cannot simply undo his society's contradictions. The novel ends with the possibility of renewed exchange rather than a completed revolution. His journey has not revealed a perfect world; it has reopened the possibility of change.

## Characters and Places to Remember

- **Shevek:** Anarresti physicist whose work on time and communication brings him into conflict with both societies.
- **Takver:** Shevek's partner, a biologist and a central source of companionship, practical insight, and commitment.
- **Sabul:** Senior physicist on Anarres who uses professional influence to control Shevek's access to scholarship and recognition.
- **Bedap:** Shevek's childhood friend and later political ally, who helps articulate the ways informal conformity can suppress freedom.
- **Odo:** Revolutionary philosopher whose ideas inspired the settlement of Anarres; her writings remain influential but are interpreted in conflicting ways.
- **Vea:** A wealthy Urrasti woman whose interactions with Shevek expose differences in class, gender, and social expectations.
- **Chifoilisk:** A physicist from the rival state of Thu who warns Shevek about the political interests behind his reception on Urras.
- **Keng:** Terran ambassador who offers Shevek a broader perspective on the two worlds and helps him share his discovery.
- **Anarres:** Resource-poor moon organized around Odonian anarchism, mutual aid, and the rejection of ownership.
- **Urras:** Fertile, wealthy planet divided among competing political and economic systems, including A-Io and Thu.

## Themes and Ideas

### An ambiguous utopia

Anarres is neither exposed as a fraud nor presented as a flawless solution. Its people have built a society without many of Urras's formal inequalities, but habit, reputation, and bureaucracy can still constrain individual lives. The novel asks how a revolution can remain a living practice rather than harden into orthodoxy.

### Freedom, obligation, and ownership

Both worlds claim to value freedom while defining it differently. Urras protects certain individual privileges within a system of ownership and inequality; Anarres rejects property but can make collective expectations coercive. Shevek's challenge is to preserve mutual responsibility without surrendering independence.

### Walls, boundaries, and exchange

The wall around the Anarresti spaceport is a recurring image: it can be understood as keeping outsiders out or inhabitants in. Physical and intellectual barriers protect communities, but they can also isolate them. Shevek's voyage challenges the idea that loyalty requires separation.

### Time and human connection

Shevek's physics explores sequence and simultaneity, while the novel itself alternates between two periods of his life. The structure invites the reader to understand his departure and return together. His scientific concern with time echoes his personal concern with separation, continuity, and reunion.

### Science as a common good

Shevek resists the attempt to turn his research into a strategic asset or private possession. Sharing the theory is both an intellectual and a political act: a way to make communication possible without granting any one state ownership of the future.
