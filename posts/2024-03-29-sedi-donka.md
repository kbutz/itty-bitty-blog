We added another new tune post for Friday, March 29th. Sedi Donka is a traditional Bulgarian tune with a complex rhythm and rich background.

## Origins and Background

Sedi Donka originates in the Thrace region around the towns of Pazardzhik and Plovdiv in south-central Bulgaria. The name of the dance derives from the first line of the accompanying song, "Sedi Donka na dyukyanche," which translates to "Donka is sitting in her shop" (or simply "Donka is sitting").

## Rhythm Breakdown

The tune is played in a complex, asymmetrical meter of 25/8 (or 25/16). This long meter essentially combines two very popular rhythms:
- Two measures of the "Macedonian Seven" rhythm (7/8, counted as slow-quick-quick or 3-2-2)
- One measure of a Kopanitsa rhythm (11/8, counted as quick-quick-slow-quick-quick or 2-2-3-2-2)

When combined, the rhythm breaks down as 7/8 + 7/8 + 11/8, which yields 3-2-2, 3-2-2, 2-2-3-2-2.

## The Dance

Sedi Donka is classified as a *Krivo Horo* (which translates to "crooked dance"). The term "crooked" refers to:
- The irregular and composed dance meter.
- The two-end character of the dance, meaning the line often moves in both directions and the leaders at both ends may draw the line into a curve.

Dancers typically form short lines holding belts ("na lesa"), with the right arm under. The steps themselves are energetic, sharp, and bouncy.
